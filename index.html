Case 01

Freelance business analyst · Freedom Pools, Adelaide SA · 2026

Should a pool builder open a partnership channel with home builders?

Engaged by Freedom Pools, working with the area manager and the sales manager, to establish whether a builder and display-village channel was worth pursuing, and to put the case in writing. The answer was yes, but smaller and more targeted than the statewide numbers suggested.

Problem

Freedom Pools needed a defensible view of whether a partnership channel with home builders and display villages justified investment, and what it would be worth if it did. I worked with the area manager and the sales manager to scope the question and test the assumptions against what they were already seeing in the market.

Data

South Australian Government housing sources: the $2.5 billion state housing package and its target of 13,500 new homes a year, 13,514 completions in 2025, and 14,863 approvals in the year to February 2026. Published Roy Morgan pool and spa ownership figures gave the adoption benchmark.

Approach

  • Built the market sizing from primary government sources rather than industry estimates.
  • Took Roy Morgan's 13–14% of Australians in a home with a pool or spa as the adoption benchmark, and set out why that is a ceiling, not a like-for-like rate for new builds.
  • Narrowed the target market to the northern growth corridors of Riverlea, Angle Vale, Roseworthy and Playford, where 94% of homes built between 2017 and 2021 were detached. The statewide figure had been overstating the opportunity.
  • Modelled revenue and gross profit across a grid of adoption and market-share scenarios, then ran sensitivity checks to find which assumption actually moved the result.
  • Specified the CRM fields needed to measure the channel once live: referral source, estate, build stage, quote value, sale status, lost reason, margin and install year.

What came out

A written case with a scenario grid instead of a single number, and a recommendation to run a small pilot with three to five builders against clear success measures, rather than standing up a full division on an untested assumption.

What I took from it

The statewide adoption rate was the seductive number and the wrong one. Most of the value in the engagement came from narrowing the denominator and being explicit about which assumption the answer actually hangs on.

Case 02 Live

Product & web · akasaretreat.com · Attappadi, Kerala

AKASA Valley Retreat: a land release site built to qualify enquiries, not just look good

Built and launched the public website for a 39-lot land development, with one job: bring in buyer enquiries and qualify them before they reach a human.

Problem

A development that isn't built yet has to be marketed honestly and still convert. Almost all traffic arrives on a phone, through a link forwarded on WhatsApp.

Approach

  • Laid the site out to follow how a buyer actually decides: the valley and masterplan first, then plot detail and remaining availability, with enquiry capture as the single call to action.
  • Put the remaining lot count on the page (39 released, 15 still available) so the scarcity is visible rather than claimed.
  • Built the SEO and social layer: canonical URLs, meta descriptions, Open Graph and Twitter card tags with a custom cover image, so forwarded links render properly.
  • Mobile first throughout, because that's where the traffic is.
  • Worked the approval and timeline caveats into the copy so the marketing stayed accurate about a development still in progress.

What I took from it

Constraint is a design tool. Publishing the real remaining-lot count is both the most honest thing on the page and the most persuasive.

Case 03 Live app

Data visualisation · Adelaide University · R Shiny · 2025–2026

SA Crime Analytics Dashboard

An interactive dashboard over South Australian open crime data, so a user can filter by offence type, period and location without writing a line of code.

Data

SAPOL open crime records for FY 2023–24, 123,837 incidents, joined to suburb-level shape files for spatial analysis.

Approach

  • Cleaned and reshaped the incident records in R with dplyr and tidyr.
  • Built a four-section Shiny app: Overview, Crime Patterns, Concentration, and an interactive map.
  • Composed monthly trend charts, offence heatmaps, a Pareto view and a Leaflet choropleth on a log-scale intensity ramp.
  • Handled the pipeline end to end: ingestion, cleaning, geocoding and aggregation, with scripts that rerun cleanly and assumptions written down.

Findings

  • Monthly incidents peaked at 11,380 in January 2024.
  • Theft was the dominant offence category in every month of the year.
  • The Pareto view showed the top categories accounting for 80% of reported crime; the choropleth singled out the Adelaide CBD at 8,521 incidents.

What I took from it

Skewed spatial counts drown a linear colour scale. Switching the choropleth to log-scale intensity is what made the suburb differences readable at all.

Case 04

Machine learning · Adelaide University · Python & R · 2025–2026

SA Crime Hotspot Prediction

Predicting which suburbs land in the top band by total offences, early enough to be useful for a resourcing decision.

Approach

  • Engineered temporal and location features per suburb: rolling mean, slope and percentage change.
  • Compared Logistic Regression, Random Forest and XGBoost under one stratified cross-validation protocol, so the comparison was like for like.
  • Reported ROC-AUC, precision, recall and F1, then tuned for recall on the basis that a missed hotspot costs more than a false alarm.
  • Wrote the results up in plain language for a non-technical audience.

What I took from it

With an imbalanced positive class, the evaluation design carries the project. Stratification and threshold-aware reporting mattered more than which algorithm won.
